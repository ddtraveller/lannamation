# The sabai weaves, in the sources' own words

The weaves of `SABAI_WEAVES` in `kit/weaving.js` (owner, 2026-10-28: "Capture as many authentic weaves as you can",
then "Just make as many sabai as you can for the kit. Assign one to each human in the kit with a sabai"). They were
found three ways: a search of the 25 sources of the NotebookLM notebook "Lanna Thai weaving iconography"
(`268fba1d-6fd8-4bca-8aec-0753a056f560`), a search of journals, theses and museum pages on the web, and a deep research
of the same notebook. A cloth is in the kit only where a source describes how it looks; each quotation below was
found in its source's own text. The sources are in copyright, so each gets a short quotation and its link. The Thai of
a PDF is given with its tone marks put back in order.

What the kit adds where a source is silent (most often the colours) is said in each weave's `from`, and below.

## Found and left out

- Not a cloth worn on the shoulder: the Tai Lue pha chet luang (a temple banner) and pha chet noi (a small cloth for
  the face, the bow and the offerings); the Chiang Kham pha hom tam kao and the Hat Siao red and black khit wraps and
  pha hom jong (body wraps and blankets); the monks' shoulder cloths.
- Not of the north: the Phu Tai praewa, the Tai Song Dam pha piao, the Lao Khrang sabai of the fig flower, the Udon
  Thani khit pha biang, the Tai Phuan of Ban Chiang and of Xieng Khouang.
- Too little said of how it looks to draw: the court ladies' silk wrap (only its silk), the Tai Yai court sash
  (embroidered with silver and gold, its ground not given), the old Luang Prabang pha biang found in Nan (only its
  silver and gold threads), the Lanna women's light neck cloth.
- Modern products: the Xayabury Cotton scarves, the mo hom indigo scarves of Phrae.
- Not read: the British Museum's Tai Lue pha chet (As1910,-.56 and -.57) and the Asian Art Museum's pha biang, whose
  pages refused the fetch.
