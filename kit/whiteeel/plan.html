The White Eel ตำนานปลาไหลเผือกHow the city of Yonok became the Nong Lom marsh: a Lanna Tales cartoon, told in Kam Mueang. Proposed script of 5 November 2026.

A prince from far away founds a city by the Kok river, on the advice of a Brahman who is really the naga Phanthu: "have a heart that loves people and animals." Forty-five kings later, the people catch a giant white eel, drag it to the king, and share it through every house but one: an old widow's, whom no one feeds. That evening a young man from nowhere asks to stay with her, eats nothing, and tells her not to come down from her house until she sees his face again. In the night the city roars three times. In the morning there is only water, and her house. The village heads say they were wrong to look down on the old, take her in, and choose a leader together. Who the young man was, no one knows.

14 scenes, the tale 10:20 long with 58 narration lines; then about 150 s of credits. With the hero film (97.75 s) and the studio card (12 s) first, the whole film is 14:39.

The choices in this script

Owner, 5 November 2026: the fish is the white eel (ปลาไหลเผือก), as people know it today; the young man stays a mystery, with a hint that he could be a naga. Elsewhere this script follows the Singhanavati chronicle (ตำนานสิงหนวติกุมาร), the oldest written telling, read in its 1936 typed text on Thai Wikisource. Pan's two questions about these points are still open in her toolset; if she answers otherwise, the lines marked below change.

The hint, and no more. The chronicle names a naga only at the founding: the Brahman who tells the prince where to build is the naga Phanthu in disguise, and he digs the city's bounds in his own shape. The film shows that, so when another stranger comes to the widow at the end, the viewer can wonder. As the young man leaves, he walks down toward the river in the dark, and something long and pale gleams once under the water: a ripple, not a tail. At the end the narrator asks who he was, says some people think he was a naga, and that no one knows.
