# Nature truth for the Doi Khao Khwai film (lowland Mueang Chiang Rai, about 400-500 m)

Researched 2026-10-27. The rule: every claim carries its source URL, and anything no source here backs is marked **UNCONFIRMED**. "General knowledge" means no source was found in this pass, so read it as UNCONFIRMED too. "Weak source" means a blog, shop or press page, not a university or agency.

Three things matter beyond "does it grow there":
1. **Native or introduced, and when.** The film has a royal palace garden of an *old* Lanna city. Some plants on the list came from the Americas and reached Thailand late: plumeria, bougainvillea, marigold, bua tong, papaya, pineapple and dragon fruit. Dragon fruit is a 1990s arrival, and bua tong a modern roadside weed. If the tale is set centuries ago, these are anachronisms.
2. **Altitude.** 400-500 m is foothill lowland. Hill evergreen forest, and the plants of the doi tops, start at about 1,000 m.
3. **Kit names that I could not tie to a species** (kettawa; and ta hern, where the name is shared) are flagged.

Main sources used:
- **TNP** = Thai National Parks species pages (thainationalparks.com). Each page gives the Bird Conservation Society of Thailand (BCST) status and a list of districts with records. The site says its maps are "based on limited data" and may be incomplete, so a missing district does not prove the bird is absent.
- **TB-CS** = Nick Upton, thaibirding.com, Chiang Saen site guide (lowland Chiang Rai, Mekong, Nong Bong Khai lake, Nam Kam, Yonok): https://thaibirding.com/locations/north/chiangsaen.htm

"Mueang CR listed" means TNP lists a record in Mueang Chiang Rai District, which contains Doi Khao Khwai.

### Bird season notes for the scenes

- **Rainy, Jul-Aug (transplanting):** use openbill, cattle egret, red-wattled lapwing, black-collared starling, olive-backed sunbird, spot-billed and whistling ducks. Chinese pond heron and grey-headed lapwing are winter birds: leave them out.
- **Cold to dry, Nov-Feb (harvest, mist):** add Chinese pond heron, grey-headed lapwing flocks and little ringed plover on drained fields. At Chiang Saen the wintering wildfowl peak in Dec-Mar.
- **Hot, Mar-Apr, and early rains:** red-wattled lapwings nesting (Mar-Aug), black-collared starlings building (Feb-May), Oriental pratincoles arriving to nest on dry fields (exact months UNCONFIRMED), olive-backed sunbirds nesting (Apr-Aug).

## The rice calendar near Chiang Rai

From the Rice Department's Chiang Rai Rice Research Center field surveys (their own words, read from the PDFs):

- **Planting (Jun-Aug).** Survey of **20 July 2023**: in irrigated areas most farmers had finished planting. Rainfed fields were still being prepared, waiting for water. Varieties: KDML105 (jasmine), **RD6 (sticky rice)**, RD15, San Pa Tong 1 and K.Wor.Kor 2. Methods today: **hand transplanting**, wet broadcasting, machine transplanting, seedling throwing and drill seeding. In **Mueang Chiang Rai, birds ate the seed after broadcasting**, so farmers sowed a second time. One Phan plot sown on 20 June was tillering on 20 July, in 7.6 cm of water. At 9 am the sky was clear, the air cool and **hazy with smoke**. https://newwebs2.ricethailand.go.th/upload/doc/9626/1691459282.pdf
- **Heading to harvest (Oct-Nov).** Survey of **17-18 October 2022**: across Chiang Rai most rice was **flowering to heading**. Some areas were already harvested; the **Mueang Chiang Rai plot was already harvested**. A KDML105 plot sown on 9 May was flowering on 18 October. The report notes a **cold air mass with morning fog** in mid-October. https://newwebs2.ricethailand.go.th/upload/doc/6414/1666853853.pdf
- So, for the film: green paddies Jul-Sep; heading and turning gold in October; harvest late October to November. Traditional hand-threshing, straw stacks and the months they stand: **UNCONFIRMED** (no source found).
- **Dec-Feb, after the rice.** Chiang Rai farmers grow **soybean in the dry season after rice**. In one Chiang Saen study, **95.83% prepared the land by cutting the rice stubble, spreading it and burning it**. The Department of Agriculture has run dry-season soybean trials in Chiang Rai. Marigold is a post-rice field crop in neighbouring Phayao. Tobacco, garlic and a second rice crop for Mueang Chiang Rai: **UNCONFIRMED**. https://repository.stou.ac.th/items/3bf7de24-71c2-4c0d-87ef-9c65a8044014 ; https://www.doa.go.th/plan/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/378ทดสอบเทคโนโลยีเพื่อเพิ่มประสิทธิภาพการผลิตถั่วเหลืองฤดูแล้ง-จังหวัดเชียงราย.pdf ; https://www.matichon.co.th/sme/news_75411

- **Cold season.** The Chiang Rai city municipality gives mid-October to mid-February, mean minimum **15.1 °C**, lowest **10.2 °C** (https://www.chiangraicity.go.th/content/general.pdf). In cold spells the lowland of Mueang Chiang Rai has measured **6 °C**, while Phu Chi Fa had frost (https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9540000133181). TMD named Chiang Rai among the "very cold" provinces (below 8 °C) for Dec 2024-Jan 2025 (https://www.tmd.go.th/media/agro/seasonal/2567/พยากรณอากาศเกษตรฤดหนาว2567.pdf). **Morning fog** is already noted in mid-October (Rice Department, above). Thick morning cloud seas are a feature of the hilltops (e.g. Doi Pha Tang). Mist lying in the lowland paddies at dawn: likely, but no source names it for Mueang Chiang Rai (**UNCONFIRMED**).
- **Hot season.** March and April: highs of about 34 °C, almost no rain, and **smoke haze** from field and forest burning. In 2026 Chiang Mai led IQAir's world pollution ranking at the end of March, and Chiang Rai has been among the provinces over the PM2.5 safe limit. So the light is a dull white-orange sun and hills fading into haze, not clear blue. Sources as in the rice section above.
- **Rains.** May-October, wettest Jul-Sep (above table).

## A Lanna farmer's yard

- **Northern Thai home-garden study** (*Tropics*, 2010): coconut dominated the top storey (10 m and up). The large trees were mostly four kinds: **coconut 21%, tamarind 15%, jackfruit 11%, mango 10%**. Gardens held 25-40 species each. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001204423428736
- **A northern village's woody plants** (Panyadee et al. 2016, *Economic Botany*): 94 woody species, most of them edible fruit trees, mango the commonest. https://www.infona.pl/resource/bwmeta1.element.springer-doi-10_1007-S12231-016-9348-9
- **A Lanna kitchen-garden study** exists (Mae Jo University 2020, San Sai), but its abstract names no species. https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/MJUJN/article/view/242058
- From the tables above, these also belong in or by a lowland yard: banana, sugarcane, ma khwaen (fruit drying on racks in Nov-Dec), saban nga and dok kaew for offerings and hair, a bael or longan tree, and som poi at the forest edge.
- **Leave out:** frangipani (a temple tree by old belief), and durian, mangosteen, rambutan and dragon fruit.
- Betel vine and areca palm by the house, chilli, lemongrass and galangal: general knowledge, **UNCONFIRMED** (no source found in this pass).
