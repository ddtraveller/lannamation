Hong Hin, the Stone Swan หงส์หินThe prince who rode a swan of stone: a Lanna Tales cartoon, told in Kam Mueang. Proposed script of 11 November 2026.

In Varanasi, six jealous wives steal the chief queen's newborn son, put a spotted puppy in his place and have her driven out. Indra catches the falling baby, raises him in heaven, and at thirteen sends him home with a sword that cannot break and a swan made of stone that flies. Living with his mother in a gardener's hut, the boy beats his six half-brothers at saba with red seeds, kills the giantess who eats the city's people, and lets the brothers take the credit. When the king sends his sons to bring back his own mother, stolen by ghouls twenty years before, the boy flies for them: through three giant kingdoms, where he wins three wives, to the ghoul city, where he finds his grandmother, and past the beast that swallows elephants. At the river the brothers kill him and take her home. Indra brings him back; his grandmother knows him; the truth comes out; the wives and their sons are punished severely; the king begs Vimala's forgiveness; and Hong Hin is crowned.

21 scenes, the tale 18:35 long with 99 narration lines; then about 150 s of credits. With the hero film (97.75 s) and the studio card (12 s) first, the whole film is 22:54.

The choices in this script

Owner, 11 November 2026: tell the tale as the old text tells it, naturally, at whatever length it takes; do not judge the other culture by another's standards; the ghouls eat their meat raw, as the story has it; only the beheading of the six wives and their sons is not shown: "just say they were punished severely". The telling followed is Chao Suriyawong's khao so (ค่าวหงส์หิน, 1901), with the palm-leaf Jataka where they agree; its names are the khao so's, as the tale's NotebookLM notebook quotes them.

The owner's answers to the plan's four questions, 11 November 2026: the killings (the giantess, the ghouls, the Hemmarat) are shown as the stroke, then a cut to the body already fallen, no head seen coming off; the sign of the milk is shown as the text tells it; the three princesses' vow is as the text: a fire, and leaping into it to die with him; the hero is named once, when his mother names him, then "the boy".
