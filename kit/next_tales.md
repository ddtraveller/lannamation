# The next tales: eight candidates, and what the kit needs to draw them

Written 2026-10-25, after Maeng and Chang Ngu (owner: "Write those stories down as a list and kick off a notebooklm
notebook about each. Catalog what the kit needs to render all the stories"). The tales come from the research
notebook "Lanna fairy tales and mythology", asked for the best tales for a family film of about 17 minutes, and its
sources checked: most rest on one short transcript of a modern Thai cartoon retelling (the Lanna Folktales series of
the Thai Media Fund), 1,200 to 5,700 characters. Each tale now has a NotebookLM notebook of its own, its old texts
added and a deep web search started. None is chosen yet; the owner picks. `TODO.md` is the older wishlist from the
seventeen tales; this list says, tale by tale, what the kit has and what it lacks.

"Kit only" is the respect rule of `TODO.md`: the monkhood, relics and funeral rites stay out of the cartoon maker.

## The tales

1. **Phaya Mangrai founds Chiang Mai** (ตำนานพญามังราย). Chiang Saen, Chiang Rai, Lamphun, Wiang Kum Kam, Chiang
   Mai. A queen of Ngoen Yang (Chiang Saen) dreams a star falls into her room; her son Mangrai becomes king at
   twenty, founds Chiang Rai on the Kok at a full moon, and takes Haripunchai (Lamphun) without a siege through his
   friend Ai Fa, who goes in as a merchant and turns the city from its king. He makes peace with Ngam Mueang of
   Phayao and Phra Ruang of Sukhothai, builds Wiang Kum Kam and is flooded out of it, and with his two friends
   lays out Chiang Mai below Doi Suthep on 12 April 1296, where he sets the city pillar. At 72, lightning takes him
   in the market. *Old texts:* the Chiang Mai chronicle (ตำนานพื้นเมืองเชียงใหม่, 1971 printing, on Commons, to
   OCR), the Jinakalamali, the chronicle of Ngoen Yang (ประชุมพงศาวดาร ๖๑). *Gaps:* the omens at Chiang Mai (white
   deer, a white mouse and her young) and Chiang Rai (a runaway elephant) are not in the notebook's sources yet.
   *Soften:* the taking of Lamphun, one account's love affair, the death.
2. **The White Eel and Wiang Nong Lom** (ตำนานปลาไหลเผือก). Yonok, Chiang Saen; the Nong Lom wetland today. The
   people of Yonok catch a white eel seven fathoms long and share its meat in every house but a widow's. A visitor
   in white warns her to stay in; in the night three quakes come, and by morning the city lies under a lake with
   her hut alone on an island. The end of Chang Ngu's city. *Old text:* the Singhanawat chronicle (ประชุมพงศาวดาร
   ๖๑, already read for Chang Ngu). *Soften:* a whole city drowns. Perhaps ten minutes of story.
3. **Hong Hin, the stone swan** (หงส์หิน). Set in Varanasi in the telling. Six jealous queens swap Queen Vimala's
   baby for a puppy and cast them out; devas raise the boy and give him a golden stone swan to fly. He wins the
   princes' saba game, kills the ogre that eats the city's people, flies over a river to free his grandmother from a
   tower in the city of ghouls, wins three ogre kings' daughters on the way, is killed by his half-brothers and
   brought back by Indra; the king learns the truth. *Old text:* the Lanna Paññāsa Jātaka manuscripts (not in the
   notebook). *Soften:* the killing, the three brides, the puppy swap.
4. **Pu Thaen Ya Thaen, the sky grandparents** (ปู่แถนย่าแถน). Hill pastures and a cave. Two orphan herders go to
   ask why their work brings nothing, and find the crystal cave where the sky grandparents weigh deeds. The kind boy
   gets his weight in gold and a white thread that leads to his bride; the greedy one gets a pea of gold. *Soften:*
   his "ugly bride" in the telling. No real place.
5. **Tao Noi Ong Kham, the little golden turtle** (เต่าน้อยอองคำ). Ban Thung Khan Chai (Thung Tao), Theng, Chiang
   Rai. A mother drowned by a second wife comes back as a golden turtle to watch her daughter Utthara; the turtle
   is cooked, Utthara buries its bones at the city gate, and they grow a golden bodhi tree only she can lift, which
   makes her queen. Her stepfamily puts a double in her place; her flower weaving shows the king the truth.
   *Soften:* the drowning, eating the turtle, the stepmother as a krasue.
6. **Pu Chao Lao Chok and Doi Tung** (ปู่เจ้าลาวจก). Doi Tung, Mae Sai, Chiang Rai. The Lua chief who clears the
   slopes with his hoe is given the Buddha's relic to keep by the king of Chiang Saen; his three sons hold the three
   peaks, the twin chedi are built and a bamboo banner a hundred wa long is raised above the clouds. *Old text:* the
   temple chronicles (ประชุมตำนานพระธาตุ, 1970, Doi Tung pp. 14-40, to OCR). Little conflict; the relic is kit only.
7. **Xiang Miang the trickster** (เซียงเมี่ยง). A clever boy tricks the king into the river by saying he could only
   trick him out of it. One joke in the notebook (1,251 characters); a film needs his other tales, which the new
   notebook's search looks for.
8. **Khatam Pa, the temple boy** (ขะต๋ำป๋าค่ำตุ๊). A temple boy made to carry the salt while the monk rides gets
   even with itching pods, a catfish and a rolling stone. A monk is the butt of the joke: against the respect rule
   unless the monk is made a village elder.
